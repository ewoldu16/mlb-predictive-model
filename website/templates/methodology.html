HOW IT WORKS

Methodology

A pregame-only count model designed around chronological evaluation rather than random train/test splits.

01

Data

MLB schedules and results, Statcast pitches, official MLB pitching boxscores, actual historical lineups, starter matchups, bullpen availability and contextual team information.

02

Temporal leakage control

Every historical feature uses information available before its game. Model evaluation expands forward through time: earlier seasons train the model and the next season validates it. Imputation and scaling are fitted on training seasons only.

03

Feature engineering

The compact model retains starter x lineup matchup, Pythagorean/team strength, bullpen quality, venue context, quality-adjusted offense and actual-lineup information.

04

Feature reduction

Permutation importance, single-feature ablation, family ablation and chronological compact-model comparisons reduced 173 inputs to 50 while preserving OOS behavior.

Forecast architecture

50 pregame features→Training medians + standardization→Poisson regression (alpha=10)→Expected team runs
Away + home means→Projected total
Negative-binomial run distributions→Winner probability + uncertainty intervals

The winner calculation conditions independent team-run distributions on a non-tied final result. This is an approximation; team scores can share game-level dependencies the model does not explicitly represent.

Limitations

  • MLB scoring contains substantial irreducible randomness.
  • Team-run rank correlation is modest, and extreme scoring explosions remain difficult to predict.
  • Confidence is useful in aggregate but not perfectly monotonic.
  • Predictions above 65% were historically sparse and unstable.
  • These are statistical estimates, not certainties.
  • 2025 was revisited during later project research and is not presented as pristine for every subsequent analysis.
